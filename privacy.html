Privacy policy

Last updated October 18, 2026

Little Us Health is a personal project by Alejandro Ponce. It serves one household of two people and has a single user who signs in with their own Google account. This page explains what it does with that person's data.

What data it accesses

With the user's permission, it reads data from their Google Health account through the Google Health API, using only two read-only scopes: sleep, and health metrics and measurements. That covers sleep sessions and stages, daily heart rate variability, resting heart rate, respiratory rate and skin temperature change. It does not write to the user's Google Health account.

How it uses the data

It uses the data for one purpose: to compute a daily freshness score for the user, shown as fresh, average, tired or stressed. The user and the other member of their household can see the score and the sleep and heart numbers behind it inside the private Little Us app. The data is not used for advertising, profiling, marketing, credit decisions or training artificial intelligence models, and no one else receives it.

Google API Services User Data Policy

Little Us Health's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Where it is stored

The raw measurements are kept on the user's own devices. The score and the numbers shown in the app are stored in the household's private database, which only the two household members can read. Data is protected by the sign-in of those two Google accounts.

How long it is kept and how to delete it

Raw measurements are kept only as long as the user wants the feature on. The user can stop it at any time by turning it off in the app or by removing access at myaccount.google.com/permissions. On request, all data collected from Google Health is deleted, usually within a week. Write to the address below.

Sharing

Data is not sold, rented or shared with third parties. It is shown only to the two members of the household, as described above.

Children

The tool is not meant for anyone under 18.
